Attention playground

A transformer of one or two layers, written here with its own backward pass, is trained in the page to reverse, sort or recall a short sequence of digits, and one can watch its attention form. In every head each position emits a query \(q\) that is compared with the keys \(k\) of the positions it may see, and the softmax of the scores weights their values \(v\); on the reverse task each position of the answer learns to look at its mirror image in the prompt, so that the matrix becomes anti-diagonal. The recall task (a b … a → b) needs two layers, since the token that follows the earlier copy of the query must first be found and then read.

Scene K of opt-playground, after C. Olah and S. Carter, Attention and Augmented Recurrent Neural Networks (2016), and Vaswani et al. (2017): the ideas are theirs, rebuilt here from the equations.
