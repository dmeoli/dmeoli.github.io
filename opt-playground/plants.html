Plants from rewriting rulesPiante da regole di riscrittura

Lindenmayer (1968) described the growth of simple multicellular organisms by rules that rewrite, in parallel and at every step, each symbol of a word into a word, as every cell of a filament divides at the same time; with brackets that open and close a branch, and with a turtle that reads the word as a sequence of moves, turns and branchings, a few rules and a few steps give the architecture of a tree, of a bush or of a weed. The plants below are those of Prusinkiewicz and Lindenmayer, The Algorithmic Beauty of Plants (Springer, 1990), chapters 1 and 2, with the rules of the book in the box at the side, which can be changed, and the thickness of every branch given by the rule of Leonardo: the cross section of a branch equals the sum of those of the branches it carries.

Lindenmayer (1968) ha descritto la crescita di semplici organismi pluricellulari con regole che riscrivono, in parallelo e a ogni passo, ciascun simbolo di una parola in una parola, come ogni cellula di un filamento si divide nello stesso istante; con parentesi che aprono e chiudono un ramo, e con una tartaruga che legge la parola come una sequenza di passi, rotazioni e ramificazioni, poche regole e pochi passi danno l'architettura di un albero, di un cespuglio o di un'erba. Le piante qui sotto sono quelle di Prusinkiewicz e Lindenmayer, The Algorithmic Beauty of Plants (Springer, 1990), capitoli 1 e 2, con le regole del libro nel riquadro a lato, che si possono cambiare, e lo spessore di ogni ramo dato dalla regola di Leonardo: la sezione di un ramo è la somma di quelle dei rami che porta.

A 3D print of this plantUna stampa 3D di questa pianta

The plant of the rules above can be ordered as a 3D print, as one closed solid, every branch a round cone joined to the others with a small fillet, the twigs never thinner than 0.6 mm in radius; standing, it is as tall as the size and grows from a disc, with the trunk up. Since a printer lays every layer on the one below, the branches that lean more than about 55 degrees from the vertical (their share is given under the view) need supports: the shrub needs few, the sympodial tree and the bush some, while the ternary tree, bent by gravity (unless its tropism is turned negative), and the monopodial tree of Honda, whose lateral branches spread almost horizontally, need many. A plant in a plane, such as the fractal plant, is better printed lying flat, as a plate that needs none. The request sends the link to the plant as it is on the screen, with its rules.
